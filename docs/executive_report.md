# Revenue growth quality and GTM performance

## Executive Summary

- **The business is still growing, but momentum is slowing.** Ending ARR reached ₹150.9 crore in December 2025, while year-over-year ARR growth declined from 71.0% in 2024 to 47.7% in 2025.
- **Customer count did not decline.** Active customers increased every observed month and finished at 489. In 2025, 163 new customers entered the portfolio and 27 customers permanently churned.
- **Growth is not only a logo-volume story.** Active-customer growth slowed to 41.3% in 2025, while average ARR per active customer increased 4.5% and post-sale motions added ₹12.0 crore.
- **Churn requires different segment actions.** Mid-Market customers starting on Professional had higher observed permanent logo churn at 8.3%, while Large Enterprise losses carried more ARR per account.

## Growth remains positive, but the rate is decelerating

ARR and active-customer counts increased throughout the 36-month period. The important tension is not contraction but slowing momentum: year-over-year ARR growth declined from 71.0% at December 2024 to 47.7% at December 2025, while active-customer growth declined from 60.2% to 41.3%. ARR continued to grow faster than customer count, supported by a 4.5% increase in average ARR per active customer during 2025.

The implication is to review absolute scale and growth rates together. A rising ARR line can coexist with weakening momentum as the installed base becomes larger.

## Monthly additions remained above permanent churn

The active-customer base increased in every observed month. During 2025, monthly new customers ranged from 11 to 16, while permanent churn ranged from zero to four. New-logo volume therefore remained above permanent churn throughout the year.

This synthetic scenario does not show customer-base contraction. It does show rising absolute churn: 27 permanent churns occurred in 2025, compared with nine in 2024. Because the portfolio is larger, monthly churn counts should be monitored alongside stable rate-based retention measures.

## Mid-Market loses more logos; Enterprise losses carry more ARR

Mid-Market customers starting on Professional recorded 25 permanent churns from 301 acquired customers, an observed full-history rate of 8.3%. Large Enterprise customers starting on Enterprise recorded 15 churns from 228 customers, or 6.6%.

Large Enterprise nevertheless lost ₹6.7 crore of ARR compared with ₹4.7 crore for Mid-Market, because each Enterprise account carried more value. Mid-Market needs broader logo-retention coverage; Enterprise needs high-touch prevention for fewer but more financially material accounts.

## Partner leads the run-rate; Outbound needs tighter quality controls

Partner led ending ARR at ₹39.5 crore. Outbound reached a similar ₹39.1 crore but had the highest observed channel logo churn at 14.1%. This is descriptive rather than causal, but it supports protecting Partner capacity and investigating Outbound qualification, integration fit, and implementation readiness.

## Net retention masks gross leakage

The 2025 cohort expanded to 100.7% of starting MRR, but GRR was only 94.4%. Expansion and upgrades repaired the aggregate, while permanent churn still removed revenue. Missing integrations and low adoption together accounted for 60.8% of modeled ARR lost, pointing toward early technical adoption as the most actionable retention theme.

## Recommended next steps

1. Review ARR growth, active-customer growth, average ARR per customer, new logos, permanent churn, NRR, and GRR together each month.
2. Build a Mid-Market retention playbook focused on early adoption and integration activation.
3. Use high-touch intervention for high-value Enterprise customers showing integration or adoption risk.
4. Tighten Outbound qualification around integration requirements and implementation readiness.
5. Define an upgrade signal for Professional customers showing seat or integration growth.

## Further questions

- Does segment churn remain different after normalizing for tenure, industry, contract size, and acquisition channel?
- Which onboarding milestones best predict permanent churn versus temporary suspension?
- How much of average ARR-per-customer growth comes from expansion versus plan upgrades?
- How would lead volume, win rate, CAC, payback, and sales-cycle length change the channel recommendation?

## Caveats and assumptions

The entire dataset is deterministic synthetic data. ARR is a modeled MRR run-rate, not audited revenue. Channel and segment churn comparisons are descriptive full-history ratios with unequal exposure periods, not annualized churn rates. The segment denominator uses the starting plan so later upgrades do not redefine the original cohort. Funnel, CAC, quota, and sales-cycle metrics remain out of scope because the four-table model has no lead, opportunity, activity, marketing-spend, or quota facts.
