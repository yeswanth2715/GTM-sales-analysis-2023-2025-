# Revenue growth and GTM performance

## Executive Summary

- **Recurring revenue grew strongly.** Ending ARR reached ₹150.9 crore in December 2025, 152.6% above the December 2023 MRR run-rate.
- **Partner is the best modeled quality-growth channel.** It led ending ARR at ₹39.5 crore, while Outbound reached a similar ₹39.1 crore with a much higher 14.1% observed logo churn.
- **Retention is healthy but must be read with gross losses.** The 2025 starting cohort ended at 100.7% NRR and 94.4% GRR, showing that expansion offset real churn pressure.
- **Post-sale motions matter.** Expansion, upgrades, and reactivation added ₹12.0 crore of ARR during 2025; missing integrations were the largest permanent-churn reason by ARR lost.

## Partner leads the run-rate; Outbound needs tighter quality controls

Partner and Outbound together contributed just over half of ending ARR. Their quality profiles differed: Partner’s observed logo churn was 5.6%, compared with 14.1% for Outbound. This is not a causal channel test, but it is strong enough to justify a modeled action: protect Partner capacity and investigate Outbound qualification, integration fit, and implementation readiness.

## Enterprise customers and upgrades are the core revenue engine

Large Enterprise customers on the Enterprise plan produced 65.7% of ending ARR. Mid-Market customers that upgraded to Enterprise added another 10.8% of ending ARR. The implication is to operate Enterprise acquisition and Professional-to-Enterprise upgrades as connected motions rather than separate reporting silos.

## Net retention masks gross leakage

The 2025 cohort expanded to 100.7% of starting MRR, but GRR was only 94.4%. Expansion and upgrades repaired the aggregate, while permanent churn still removed revenue. Missing integrations and low adoption together accounted for 60.8% of modeled ARR lost, pointing toward early technical adoption as the most actionable retention theme.

## Recommended next steps

1. Keep Partner and Events focused on Enterprise accounts; add real pipeline and spend data before reallocating budget.
2. Tighten Outbound qualification around integration requirements and implementation capacity.
3. Instrument onboarding milestones and integration activation in the first six months.
4. Define an upgrade propensity signal for Professional customers showing seat or integration growth.
5. Review NRR beside GRR and permanent-churn reasons every month.

## Further questions

- Does channel quality remain after normalizing for customer tenure, industry, and initial contract size?
- Which onboarding milestones best predict permanent churn versus temporary suspension?
- How do CAC, payback, sales-cycle length, and win rate change the channel recommendation?

## Caveats and assumptions

The entire dataset is deterministic synthetic data. ARR is a modeled MRR run-rate, not audited revenue. Channel comparisons are descriptive and have different exposure periods. Funnel, CAC, quota, and sales-cycle metrics are out of scope because the four-table model has no lead, opportunity, activity, marketing-spend, or quota facts.
